That is an excellent question! This is a very common issue when hosting multiple web applications on the exact same domain.

Here is exactly why this happens:

The "Shared Drawer" Problem (localStorage)
When your browser saves your login token, it saves it in a tiny storage space called localStorage. The rule of localStorage is that it is grouped by the domain name, not by the specific URL path.

Because all your apps (/power_tracker, /cctv, /water_tracker, /food_tracker) live on the exact same domain (insight.rathinamtechzone.com), they all share the exact same storage drawer.

Right now, in the frontend code for all these apps, the login token is being saved under the generic name "token".

Here is the exact chain of events:

You log into Water Tracker. The browser saves: token = "water_token_123"
You open a new tab and log into Power Tracker. The browser overwrites that exact same slot: token = "power_token_999"
You go back to your Water Tracker tab and click a button. Water Tracker grabs the token from the drawer ("power_token_999") and sends it to the Water Tracker server.
The Water Tracker server looks at it and says, "I don't recognize this token! You must be a hacker or your session expired!" and logs you out immediately.
The Fix: Namespacing
To fix this, the frontend code in each application needs to stop using generic names like "token" or "theme".

Instead, each app needs to save its data with a unique prefix, like this:

Water Tracker: localStorage.setItem('water_token', data.token)
Power Tracker: localStorage.setItem('power_token', data.token)
CCTV: localStorage.setItem('cctv_token', data.token)
This ensures they all have their own designated spot in the drawer and never overwrite each other!
